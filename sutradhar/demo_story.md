## Chapter 1: The Letter

Meera Sood read the letter twice under the flickering lamp of the Shimla guesthouse. Her green eyes stung from the smoke of the wood stove. Her little brother Arjun, seventeen and restless, paced behind her.

"Dadi wants us home by Friday," he said.

Meera folded the letter and slid it into her notebook. It was Monday, and the hills were already turning blue with dusk. She had been a crime reporter for six years, and she knew the sound of a family hiding something.

## Chapter 2: Dadi's Last Evening

They reached the old house in Kasauli on Wednesday. Dadi lay propped against the pillows, her silver braid over one shoulder, her breath thin as paper. She pressed a brass key into Meera's palm and whispered a single word: "Cellar."

By midnight Dadi was dead. Arjun wept in the corridor while Inspector Rawat wrote slowly in his notebook, glancing at the closed bedroom door.

## Chapter 3: The Cellar

On Thursday morning Meera and Arjun, who was nineteen and still pretending he did not need her, forced the cellar door. Behind the shelves of pickle jars they found a tin box of photographs. Rawat watched from the stairs, tapping his pen against the rail.

One photograph showed their grandfather on a railway platform in Delhi, laughing beside a man none of them knew. At dawn Meera caught the six o'clock train to Delhi to show the photographs to an archivist.

## Chapter 4: Tea

At nine that same morning Meera stood in the kitchen of the Kasauli house. Dadi set a cup of tea in front of her, humming an old film song. Meera's brown eyes widened; the tea tasted exactly as it always had.

Dadi tapped the photograph and said, "Your grandfather never went to Delhi."

## Chapter 5: What Stays Buried

Rawat closed his notebook and sighed. "Some secrets," he said, "stay in the cellar."

Meera pocketed the brass key and looked at Arjun, who stood a little taller than she remembered. Outside, the monsoon began to fall on the hills.
